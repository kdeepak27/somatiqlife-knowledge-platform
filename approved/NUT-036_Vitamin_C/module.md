## Definition
Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) is an essential water-soluble vitamin important for enzymatic reactions, antioxidant defense, and nutrient absorption, which must be obtained through diet due to lack of endogenous synthesis.

## Why This Matters
Adequate vitamin C status is crucial for maintaining healthy connective tissue, preventing deficiency diseases like scurvy, supporting immune function, and enhancing iron absorption, which collectively contribute to overall health and disease prevention.

## Vitamin C Biochemistry and Functions
Vitamin C is a potent biological molecule involved primarily as a cofactor for enzymes that catalyze hydroxylation reactions essential for collagen synthesis. It contributes electrons to convert proline and lysine residues into hydroxyproline and hydroxylysine, stabilizing collagen triple helices. Furthermore, vitamin C functions as an antioxidant by donating electrons to neutralize reactive oxygen species, thus protecting cells from oxidative damage linked to aging and chronic diseases.

## Collagen Synthesis and Tissue Health
Collagen is a structural protein fundamental to skin, blood vessels, cartilage, and bones. Vitamin C-dependent hydroxylation of collagen peptides ensures proper folding, stability, and cross-linking of collagen fibers. Insufficient vitamin C disrupts collagen formation, leading to fragile blood vessels, poor wound healing, and connective tissue abnormalities evident in scurvy.

## Antioxidant Role and Immune Support
As an antioxidant, vitamin C scavenges free radicals and regenerates other antioxidants like vitamin E, which reduces oxidative stress and inflammation. This action supports immune defense mechanisms by protecting leukocytes from oxidative damage during pathogen response.

## Vitamin C and Iron Absorption
Vitamin C enhances the bioavailability of non-heme iron by reducing ferric (Fe3+) to ferrous (Fe2+) iron, which is more readily absorbed in the intestine. This helps prevent iron deficiency anemia, especially in populations consuming plant-based diets with limited heme iron sources.

## Vitamin C Deficiency and Clinical Consequences
Vitamin C deficiency causes scurvy, characterized by symptoms such as fatigue, gum inflammation, bleeding, petechiae, joint pain, and impaired wound healing. These clinical signs result from defective collagen and weakened connective tissue. Early identification and supplementation prevent progression to severe disease and complications.

## Dietary Sources and Recommended Intake
Fruits like oranges, kiwifruit, strawberries, and vegetables such as bell peppers and broccoli are rich natural sources of vitamin C. Recommended daily intake for adults is approximately 75 mg for women and 90 mg for men. Cooking can reduce vitamin C content, so fresh or lightly cooked foods are preferable.

## Vitamin C Supplementation and Safety Considerations
While moderate supplementation can prevent deficiency, excessive vitamin C intake (>2000 mg/day) may cause gastrointestinal upset (diarrhea, cramps) and increase risk of kidney stones in susceptible individuals. There is limited evidence that high-dose supplementation prevents or treats chronic diseases. Caution is advised with high doses, especially in patients with predispositions.

## Common Misconceptions and Clinical Pearls
Vitamin C is often believed to prevent or cure the common cold, but evidence only suggests modest symptom duration reduction. It is not a substitute for balanced nutrition or medical treatment. Clinical signs of deficiency are rare in developed countries but should be considered in malnutrition or malabsorption cases. Vitamin C testing can aid diagnosis but is not routinely required.

## Practical Guidance
- Include vitamin C-rich fruits and vegetables daily to meet recommended intake.
- Consume vitamin C foods raw or lightly cooked to preserve content.
- Be cautious with high-dose vitamin C supplements; consult healthcare provider if considering them.
- Recognize symptoms of deficiency, especially in at-risk populations like older adults or those with limited diets.
- Avoid over-reliance on vitamin C supplements for infection prevention; maintain overall balanced nutrition.

## Safety Notes
- Excessive vitamin C intake (>2000 mg daily) can cause gastrointestinal discomfort.
- High doses may increase risk of kidney stones in people with a history of oxalate stones.
- Individuals with certain conditions (e.g., kidney disorders) should consult healthcare providers before supplementation.
- Vitamin C supplements can interfere with some medical tests; inform providers accordingly.

## Common Misconceptions
### Vitamin C can prevent or cure the common cold.
**Reality:** Vitamin C may only modestly reduce cold duration; it does not prevent or cure it.

### High doses of vitamin C are always beneficial for health.
**Reality:** Excessive doses may cause side effects, and benefits beyond preventing deficiency are unproven.

### Vitamin C deficiency is common in developed countries.
**Reality:** Clinical deficiency is rare with normal diets but can occur in malnutrition or absorption disorders.

## Clinical Pearls
- Vitamin C is essential for stable collagen to prevent tissue fragility.
- Antioxidant activity supports immune cell function during infections.
- Dietary vitamin C enhances iron absorption, reducing anemia risk in vegetarian diets.
- Deficiency symptoms typically resolve rapidly with supplementation.
- No reliable evidence supports vitamin C supplementation to prevent chronic diseases in healthy individuals.

## Key Takeaways
- Vitamin C is an essential nutrient required for collagen synthesis, antioxidant defense, and iron absorption.
- Dietary intake from fruits and vegetables typically meets requirements for most adults.
- Deficiency leads to scurvy, a rare but serious condition with recognizable clinical signs.
- High-dose supplementation has limited benefits and potential risks.
- Maintain balanced nutrition rather than relying on supplements alone.

## Glossary
- **Vitamin C:** A water-soluble vitamin (ascorbic acid) essential for collagen synthesis, antioxidant activity, and nutrient absorption.
- **Collagen:** A structural protein providing strength and support to connective tissues, skin, and bones.
- **Antioxidant:** A molecule that inhibits oxidation and neutralizes harmful free radicals to protect cells.
- **Scurvy:** A disease caused by vitamin C deficiency leading to impaired collagen production and tissue fragility.
- **Non-heme iron:** The form of iron present in plant-based foods, less readily absorbed than heme iron from animal sources.
