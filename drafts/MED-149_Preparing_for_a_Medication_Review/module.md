## Definition
A medication review is a systematic assessment of all medications a person uses, with the goal of optimizing the effectiveness, safety, and adherence of drug therapy, while minimizing adverse effects and interactions.

## Why This Matters
Medication errors, adverse drug reactions, and drug interactions are common causes of hospital admissions and poor health outcomes globally. Medication reviews help identify these issues early, ensuring safer and more effective medication use, improving overall health and quality of life.

## What Is a Medication Review?
A medication review is a clinical process in which a healthcare professional evaluates a patient's complete list of medications and supplements to ensure the treatment is appropriate, effective, and safe. This assessment looks for issues such as unnecessary medications, incorrect dosages, potential drug interactions, and adherence problems. It involves communication with the patient and/or caregivers to accurately gather medication histories and experiences with side effects or benefits.

## Mechanisms of Medication Reviews
Medication reviews function by analyzing pharmacological principles including pharmacodynamics (how drugs affect the body) and pharmacokinetics (how the body absorbs, distributes, metabolizes, and excretes drugs). Reviewing these mechanisms allows healthcare professionals to predict and prevent adverse drug events, identify interactions, and adjust therapy to individual patient physiology and health status.

## Why Prepare for a Medication Review?
Preparation ensures the medication review is thorough and accurate. Patients should collect all medication containers, including prescription pills, over-the-counter drugs, vitamins, and herbal products. Recording any missed doses, side effects, or concerns allows healthcare providers to personalize recommendations better. Preparation helps avoid incomplete or missed information that could compromise safety and effectiveness.

## Common Medication-Related Issues Identified
Medication reviews commonly detect problems such as drug-drug interactions that can alter effects or cause harm, duplicate therapies that unnecessarily increase medication burden, inappropriate medications for certain ages or conditions, non-adherence to prescribed regimens, and medication-induced side effects or toxicity. Early identification helps prevent hospitalizations and improves quality of life.

## Healthcare Professionals Involved in Reviews
Medication reviews are often conducted by pharmacists, physicians, nurses, or multidisciplinary teams, each bringing expertise in pharmacology and clinical care. Pharmacists play a unique role in identifying drug-related problems and educating patients. Collaboration among providers ensures holistic care and consensus on medication adjustments.

## Tools for Monitoring Side Effects and Interactions
Patients and providers use tools like medication lists, journals to track symptoms or side effects, electronic health records, and clinical decision-support systems to identify and monitor adverse drug reactions or interactions. Some digital apps allow patients to document and share experiences with their healthcare team, facilitating timely interventions.

## Safety Considerations During Medication Review
Major safety concerns include avoiding abrupt discontinuation of essential medications, managing withdrawal risks, and gradual dosage adjustments. Thorough assessment prevents adverse outcomes such as toxicity or disease exacerbation. Close follow-up after changes is necessary to monitor for efficacy and side effects, ensuring safe transitions in therapy.

## Limitations and Challenges in Medication Reviews
Challenges include incomplete medication histories due to patient recall limitations, underreporting of non-prescription drug use, time constraints in clinical settings, and variable provider expertise. Additionally, some drug interactions or side effects may be difficult to detect. Continuous education and standardized protocols help overcome these limitations.

## Misconceptions About Medication Reviews
A common misconception is that medication reviews only apply to elderly patients or those with multiple medications. In reality, all patients taking medication, including younger adults and those on few drugs, can benefit. Another misconception is that the review always leads to medication discontinuation; the primary goal is optimization, which may involve continuation or changes.

## How to Communicate Effectively in a Medication Review
Patients should be open about all medications and supplements they use, including adherence issues and any experienced side effects. Asking questions about the purpose and potential risks of each medication improves understanding and engagement. Clear communication ensures that healthcare providers can tailor recommendations appropriately to patient needs.

## Practical Guidance
- Compile a comprehensive list of all medications, including doses and frequency.
- Bring medication containers or a printed list to appointments for accuracy.
- Note any side effects, missed doses, or changes in symptoms since starting medications.
- Be honest about adherence and use of non-prescription products.
- Prepare a list of questions about your medications or concerns.
- Communicate any changes in health status or new diagnoses to your provider.
- Follow up as recommended after medication changes to monitor effects.
- Use medication tracking tools or apps to maintain accurate records.

## Safety Notes
- Never stop or change medication doses without consulting a healthcare professional.
- Report any new or unusual symptoms promptly during or after medication changes.
- Inform healthcare providers about all supplements and over-the-counter products used.
- Be aware of potential side effects and interactions unique to your medications.
- Ensure that monitoring tests (e.g., blood levels, kidney function) are done as advised to detect adverse effects early.

## Glossary
- **Medication Review:** A process of evaluating all medications a patient uses to ensure they are appropriate, safe, and effective.
- **Drug-Drug Interaction:** A situation when one medication affects the activity of another, potentially causing harm or reduced effectiveness.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The study of how drugs affect the body, including mechanisms of action and effects.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** The study of how the body absorbs, distributes, metabolizes, and excretes drugs.
- **Adverse Drug Event (ADE):** An injury resulting from medication use, including side effects and overdoses.
- **Medication Adherence:** The extent to which a patient takes medications as prescribed.
- **Polypharmacy:** The use of multiple medications by a patient, often leading to increased risk of interactions and side effects.
- **Medication Reconciliation:** A related process focused on verifying and documenting an accurate list of a patient's medications during healthcare transitions.
