## Practical Guidance
- Plan meals including a variety of grains, legumes, vegetables, fruits, and proteins each day.
- Incorporate traditional Indian spices like turmeric and cumin for added flavor and potential health benefits.
- Use whole grains such as brown rice and whole wheat over refined grains.
- Adjust portion sizes based on individual energy requirements and health goals.
- Prepare meals in advance to save time while maintaining freshness.
- Substitute ingredients thoughtfully to accommodate dietary restrictions or ingredient availability.
- Limit the use of oil and fried foods to reduce excess calories and fats.
- Store cooked meals properly in sealed containers and refrigerate promptly to ensure food safety.

## Safety Notes
- Avoid leaving cooked food at room temperature for extended periods to prevent bacterial growth.
- Reheat food thoroughly to at least 74°C (165°F) before consumption.
- Store perishable ingredients like dairy and meat under refrigeration at or below 4°C (39°F).
- Use clean utensils and sanitized surfaces during meal preparation to minimize contamination risk.
- Be cautious with spice levels if prone to gastrointestinal irritation or allergies.

## Glossary
- **Dal:** A dish made from cooked pulses such as lentils, chickpeas, or peas, often seasoned with spices.
- **Sabzi:** A vegetable dish or curry in Indian cuisine, typically made by cooking vegetables with spices and sometimes onions and tomatoes.
- **Chapati (Roti):** A type of unleavened flatbread made from whole wheat flour, commonly eaten in Indian meals.
- **Spices:** Aromatic or pungent plant substances such as turmeric, cumin, coriander, and ginger, used to flavor food.
- **Legumes:** Plants in the pea family producing pods with seeds inside, such as lentils, chickpeas, and beans; rich in protein and fiber.
- **Curcumin:** The active bioactive compound found in turmeric known for anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties.
- **Portion Control:** Managing the amount of food consumed in one meal to maintain energy balance and nutritional adequacy.
- **Glycemic Control:** The management of blood sugar levels through diet and lifestyle.
