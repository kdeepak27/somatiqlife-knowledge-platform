## Definition
Kidney function encompasses the kidneys' ability to filter blood by removing metabolic waste and toxins, maintaining fluid and electrolyte balance, regulating acid-base status, and influencing blood pressure via hormonal pathways. As people age, kidney function typically declines gradually but measurably.

## Why This Matters
Maintaining healthy kidney function is essential for overall health and longevity, especially as kidneys influence cardiovascular health through blood pressure regulation and waste excretion. Understanding how kidney function changes with age helps in early detection of potential kidney disease and in personalized management to reduce associated health risks like hypertension and heart failure.

## Overview of Kidney Function
The kidneys are vital organs that filter blood to remove metabolic waste and excess fluids, maintaining homeostasis. They regulate electrolyte levels, acid-base balance, and produce hormones like erythropoietin for red blood cell production, and renin for blood pressure control. The basic functional unit is the nephron, which filters blood to form urine. Kidney function is often assessed clinically by measuring glomerular filtration rate (GFR), which estimates how well the kidneys filter waste.

## Structural Changes in Aging Kidneys
As people age, the kidneys undergo anatomical changes including a decrease in kidney mass, loss of nephrons, and thickening or scarring of glomeruli (glomerulosclerosis). These changes reduce the number of functioning units and impair filtration capacity. Blood vessels within the kidney also thicken and stiffen, leading to decreased renal blood flow, which further compromises kidney function.

## Physiological Mechanisms of Kidney Aging
Functional decline in kidney filtration with age results from cumulative oxidative stress, inflammation, cellular senescence, and reduced regenerative capability of renal cells. The ability to concentrate urine diminishes, leading to a higher risk of dehydration. Hormonal regulation by the kidney may become less efficient, impacting blood pressure and red blood cell production.

## Measuring Kidney Function: GFR and Biomarkers
Glomerular filtration rate (GFR) is the standard clinical measure of kidney function, reflecting the volume of plasma filtered per minute. It can be estimated (eGFR) using serum creatinine levels alongside factors such as age, sex, and race. Other biomarkers like cystatin C are also used to improve accuracy. Normal adult GFR is approximately 90-120 mL/min/1.73m², but it decreases on average by 0.75 to 1 mL/min per year after age 30-40.

## Risk Factors Affecting Kidney Function With Age
Several health conditions accelerate kidney function decline beyond normal aging, including hypertension (high blood pressure), diabetes mellitus, obesity, cardiovascular disease, and exposure to nephrotoxic drugs. Lifestyle factors like smoking and poor diet also influence renal health. Early detection and management of these risk factors are critical to preserving kidney function.

## Chronic Kidney Disease and Aging
Chronic kidney disease (CKD) prevalence increases with age. CKD involves persistent abnormalities in kidney structure or function lasting more than three months and can progress to kidney failure. Older adults often have asymptomatic CKD, underscoring the importance of screening. CKD is associated with higher risks of cardiovascular events and mortality.

## Lifestyle Strategies to Support Kidney Health
Maintaining adequate hydration, a balanced diet low in sodium and processed foods, regular physical activity, and blood pressure control support healthy kidney aging. Weight management and smoking cessation further reduce the risk of kidney impairment. Avoiding unnecessary use of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) and other nephrotoxic agents is important.

## Medication Use and Kidney Function in Older Adults
Many medications are metabolized or excreted through the kidneys. Declining kidney function requires dose adjustments to avoid toxicity. Drugs such as NSAIDs, certain antibiotics, and contrast agents can harm kidneys, especially in older adults. Regular assessment of kidney function is recommended before starting or adjusting such medications.

## Hydration and Urine Concentrating Ability
Aging kidneys have a reduced ability to concentrate urine, increasing susceptibility to dehydration. Older adults may have diminished thirst sensation and can easily become dehydrated, impacting kidney function. Monitoring fluid intake and recognizing dehydration signs are essential preventive measures.

## Common Misconceptions About Kidney Aging
A common misconception is that declining kidney function is inevitable and untreatable with age. While some decline is normal, many lifestyle and medical interventions can slow progression and support kidney health. Another myth is that symptoms always occur early; kidney impairment often lacks symptoms until advanced stages.

## Safety Considerations: Avoiding Kidney Injury
Protecting kidneys involves avoiding dehydration, careful monitoring of blood pressure, cautious use of medications with potential nephrotoxicity, and timely evaluation of infections or acute illnesses. Individuals with compromised kidney function should work closely with healthcare providers to mitigate risks.

## Monitoring and Screening for Kidney Function in Older Adults
Routine screening with serum creatinine and estimated GFR is recommended for older adults, especially those with risk factors like hypertension or diabetes. Early detection enables interventions that may delay decline and prevent complications. Urine albumin testing may also assist in identifying kidney damage.

## Practical Guidance
- Keep well hydrated but avoid excessive fluid intake.
- Adopt a kidney-friendly diet low in sodium and saturated fats.
- Control blood pressure and blood sugar levels to reduce kidney strain.
- Avoid overuse of NSAIDs and nephrotoxic medications without medical advice.
- Regularly monitor kidney function through blood tests, especially if older or with risk factors.
- Maintain a healthy weight and exercise regularly.
- Quit smoking to improve kidney and cardiovascular health.
- Discuss any medication changes with your healthcare provider considering kidney function.

## Safety Notes
- Use medications cautiously and under medical supervision to prevent nephrotoxicity.
- Older adults should avoid dehydration as it may worsen kidney function.
- Do not ignore symptoms such as swelling, fatigue, or changes in urination frequency; seek medical advice.
- Avoid self-medicating with supplements or over-the-counter drugs that may affect kidney health.
- Ensure proper communication of kidney health status with all healthcare providers, especially before surgeries or contrast imaging.

## Common Misconceptions
### Declining kidney function is an unavoidable and untreatable part of aging.
**Reality:** While some decline is normal, many interventions can slow deterioration and preserve kidney health.

### Kidney disease always causes obvious symptoms early on.
**Reality:** Kidney issues are often silent until advanced; regular screening is essential for early detection.

### Drinking excessive water will improve kidney function in the elderly.
**Reality:** Overhydration can be harmful; appropriate hydration based on individual needs is important.

## Clinical Pearls
- Monitor eGFR trends rather than single values for kidney function assessment.
- Adjust medication doses in older adults based on kidney function tests.
- Encourage lifestyle modifications early to preserve kidney health with aging.
- Educate older adults on avoiding nephrotoxic agents including certain NSAIDs.
- Screen for albuminuria as a marker of kidney damage even if eGFR is normal.

## Key Takeaways
- Kidney function naturally declines with age due to nephron loss and vascular changes.
- Glomerular filtration rate (GFR) is the key measurement of renal function and decreases gradually after midlife.
- Hypertension, diabetes, and lifestyle factors can accelerate age-related kidney decline.
- Chronic kidney disease risk increases in older adults and often lacks early symptoms.
- Maintaining hydration, blood pressure control, and avoiding nephrotoxins supports kidney health.

## Glossary
- **Kidney:** Bean-shaped organs responsible for filtering blood, removing waste, and regulating fluid, electrolytes, and blood pressure.
- **Nephron:** The microscopic functional unit of the kidney that filters blood and forms urine.
- **Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR):** A clinical measure estimating how much blood the kidneys filter per minute.
- **Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD):** A long-term condition characterized by gradual loss of kidney function over months or years.
- **Serum Creatinine:** A waste product measured in blood used to estimate kidney filtering ability.
- **Nephrotoxic Drugs:** Medications or substances harmful to kidney cells or function.
- **Hypertension:** High blood pressure, a condition that strains the heart and blood vessels and affects kidney health.
- **Renin:** An enzyme secreted by kidneys that helps regulate blood pressure.
- **Erythropoietin:** A hormone produced by the kidneys that stimulates red blood cell production.
- **Hydration:** The state of having adequate fluids in the body to maintain physiological function.
