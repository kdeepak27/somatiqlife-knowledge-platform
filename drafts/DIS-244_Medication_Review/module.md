## Definition
Medication review is a systematic evaluation of a patient's medications to optimize therapeutic outcomes and minimize medication-related problems, including adverse drug reactions and drug interactions.

## Why This Matters
Medication-related problems are common causes of hospital admissions and poor health outcomes, especially among older adults and individuals with multiple chronic conditions. Medication reviews help identify and prevent such issues, improving patient safety and the effectiveness of treatments.

## What Is a Medication Review?
A medication review is a comprehensive and structured assessment of all medications a person is currently taking. This includes prescription drugs, over-the-counter medicines, herbal products, and dietary supplements. The primary goal is to ensure medications are appropriate, effective, and safe, and that the patient understands their treatment. Medication reviews are distinct from routine prescription refills, focusing instead on the overall medication regimen and identifying issues such as drug interactions, unnecessary medications, or non-adherence. This process is key to medication management and reducing medication-related harm.

## Mechanisms and Processes of Medication Review
Medication reviews involve evaluating each medication regarding its indication, dosage, effectiveness, and safety profile. Pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic principles guide this assessment by considering how drugs are absorbed, metabolized, distributed, and excreted, as well as how they affect the body. The review also checks for potential drug-drug and drug-disease interactions and assesses the patient's ability to adhere to the regimen. A variety of tools and criteria, such as the Beers Criteria for potentially inappropriate medications in older adults, support clinical decision-making during the review.

## Who Benefits from Medication Reviews?
Medication reviews are especially beneficial for older adults, individuals using multiple medications (polypharmacy), people with chronic diseases like diabetes or heart failure, and those recently hospitalized. Patients experiencing side effects, taking new medications, or managing complex medication regimens should also consider medication reviews. Vulnerable populations, including those with cognitive impairment or reduced kidney function, require special attention during reviews to prevent harm and optimize therapy.

## Evidence Supporting Medication Reviews
Established evidence demonstrates that medication reviews can reduce adverse drug events, improve medication adherence, and decrease hospital admissions related to medication problems. Randomized controlled trials and systematic reviews show benefits in clinical outcomes, particularly in elderly populations. However, the evidence strength varies depending on the healthcare setting and specific patient groups. Emerging studies highlight improvements in quality of life and economic benefits from reduced healthcare utilization, although these findings require further validation.

## Practical Steps to Prepare for a Medication Review
Patients should bring a complete list of all medications, including prescription, over-the-counter, and herbal supplements, to the review appointment. Providing information on medication-taking habits, side effects experienced, and previous drug allergies enhances the review's effectiveness. Healthcare providers prepare by reviewing clinical history and laboratory data. The session may involve medication reconciliation, discussion of goals of therapy, and personalized recommendations. Effective communication and shared decision-making improve outcomes.

## Limitations and Challenges of Medication Reviews
While medication reviews have clear benefits, limitations exist. Variability in delivery models, lack of standardized procedures, and incomplete patient information can reduce effectiveness. Some patients may have difficulty recalling all medications, leading to gaps. Not all healthcare systems have access to pharmacists or specialists trained in medication management. Additionally, some drug problems may not be identifiable without further diagnostic testing. Awareness of these limitations is important for setting realistic expectations and improving service quality.

## Common Misconceptions about Medication Reviews
A common misconception is that medication reviews are only necessary for elderly or very sick patients; however, anyone on medication can benefit, especially when starting new medicines. Another misunderstanding is that medication review is a one-time event, whereas regular reviews are often needed as health conditions change. Some believe medication reviews are solely pharmacist tasks, but doctors, nurses, and interdisciplinary teams also play vital roles. Addressing these misconceptions is crucial for broader acceptance and use of medication reviews.

## Safety Considerations in Medication Reviews
Medication reviews prioritize patient safety by identifying and resolving potentially harmful medication issues. However, safety depends on accurate information and careful monitoring of any changes made. Sudden discontinuation or modification of drugs without proper oversight may pose risks. Confidentiality of patient information must be maintained throughout the process. Education about recognizing adverse effects and when to seek help enhances safety outcomes following a review.

## Role of Pharmacists and Healthcare Providers in Medication Reviews
Pharmacists often lead medication reviews due to their expertise in pharmacology and drug interactions. Physicians, nurses, and other healthcare professionals collaborate to incorporate findings into the overall care plan. Interdisciplinary approaches improve comprehensive medication management and patient adherence. Training and certification standards for providers conducting medication reviews help ensure quality and consistency. Integration of electronic health records can facilitate efficient reviews.

## Integrating Medication Reviews into Preventive Care
Medication reviews are an important component of preventive healthcare as they reduce preventable medication-related problems that can lead to disease exacerbations or hospitalizations. Regular reviews support ongoing disease management, patient education, and optimization of therapy. Healthcare systems increasingly incorporate medication reviews into chronic disease programs and transitions of care protocols. Such integration promotes holistic care and reduces avoidable healthcare costs.

## Practical Guidance
- Keep an up-to-date list of all medications and share it with your healthcare providers.
- Request a medication review especially when starting new medications or after hospital discharge.
- Be honest about medication side effects, adherence challenges, and use of non-prescription products.
- Ask questions about why each medication is prescribed and how to take it correctly.
- Involve family members or caregivers in medication discussions if helpful.
- Use reminders or pill organizers to improve adherence as advised during the review.
- Follow up on any changes recommended during the medication review.
- Report any new symptoms or side effects promptly after medication adjustments.

## Safety Notes
- Do not stop or change medication doses without consulting your healthcare provider.
- Provide complete and accurate medication information to prevent errors.
- Ensure medication reviews are conducted by qualified healthcare professionals.
- Maintain confidentiality of your medical and medication records.
- Monitor for side effects or adverse reactions after medication changes and seek help if needed.

## Glossary
- **Medication Review:** A systematic assessment of all medications a patient is taking to optimize therapy and reduce medication-related problems.
- **Polypharmacy:** The concurrent use of multiple medications by a patient, often associated with increased risk of adverse drug interactions.
- **Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR):** An unwanted or harmful reaction experienced following the administration of a medication.
- **Medication Adherence:** The extent to which patients take medications as prescribed by their healthcare providers.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** The study of how drugs move through the body, including absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The study of the biochemical and physiological effects of drugs and their mechanisms of action.
- **Beers Criteria:** A guideline listing potentially inappropriate medications for use in older adults to reduce adverse effects.
- **Medication Reconciliation:** The process of creating the most accurate list possible of all medications a patient is taking to avoid errors.
