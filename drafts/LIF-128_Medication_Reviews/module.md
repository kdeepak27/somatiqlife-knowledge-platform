## Definition
Medication reviews are systematic evaluations of an individual's complete medication regimen by a qualified healthcare professional, aimed at optimizing therapy, enhancing safety, and supporting effective medication use.

## Why This Matters
Medication errors, adverse drug reactions, and inappropriate medication use are significant causes of hospitalizations and poor health outcomes. Medication reviews play a critical role in preventing these problems by identifying potential issues early, ensuring safe medication use, improving adherence, and supporting informed health decisions.

## What is a Medication Review?
A medication review is a comprehensive assessment conducted by healthcare providers, often pharmacists or physicians, to evaluate all medications a patient is taking. It involves verifying medications for appropriateness, effectiveness, safety, and adherence. The goal is to minimize medication-related problems such as adverse drug reactions, drug interactions, and unnecessary medication use. Medication reviews can be categorized as simple (focused on medicine list accuracy) or clinical (evaluating therapeutic outcomes). Synonyms include medication therapy review (MTR) and medication reconciliation, the latter typically referring to verifying medication lists during transitions of care.

## Who Performs Medication Reviews?
Medication reviews are usually performed by pharmacists due to their specialized knowledge of pharmacology and medications. However, physicians, nurse practitioners, and other qualified healthcare professionals also conduct them. The professional’s role is to gather relevant information about the patient's medication regimen, medical history, and lifestyle to identify and resolve potential medication-related issues effectively. Collaboration among healthcare providers is essential to ensure comprehensive care.

## How Are Medication Reviews Conducted?
The process begins with collecting a complete medication list, including prescription drugs, over-the-counter medications, dietary supplements, and herbal products. The healthcare professional assesses each for necessity, dosing, potential side effects, interactions, and adherence barriers. They also consider patient-specific factors such as age, kidney and liver function, and comorbid conditions. Recommendations may include adjusting dosages, discontinuing unnecessary medications, or adding new treatments. Patient education is a key component to enhance understanding and adherence.

## Mechanisms by Which Medication Reviews Improve Health
Medication reviews prevent harm by identifying adverse drug reactions (ADRs), drug-drug and drug-disease interactions, and inappropriate prescribing practices. They enhance medication adherence by addressing barriers such as complex regimens or misunderstandings. Reviews also promote deprescribing, which is the safe reduction or stopping of medications no longer needed. Together, these mechanisms reduce hospital admissions due to medication-related problems, lower healthcare costs, and improve quality of life.

## Evidence Supporting Medication Reviews
Numerous systematic reviews and meta-analyses indicate that medication reviews can reduce medication-related hospital admissions, improve patient knowledge, and enhance medication adherence. Evidence is strongest for older adults and those with polypharmacy. However, effectiveness varies based on the setting, the professional conducting the review, and the comprehensiveness of the intervention. Emerging evidence suggests benefits in diverse populations, but further high-quality randomized controlled trials are needed to confirm long-term outcomes.

## Practical Application: How to Prepare for a Medication Review
Patients can maximize the benefits of a medication review by preparing in advance. This involves bringing an up-to-date list of all medications, including dosages and schedules, and information about allergies or previous side effects. Preparing questions about concerns or symptoms helps guide the discussion. Open communication and honesty about medication adherence and lifestyle factors enable a tailored review. Healthcare providers may use medication review forms or checklists to facilitate the process.

## Limitations and Challenges of Medication Reviews
Despite their benefits, medication reviews face challenges like limited time during appointments, incomplete medical records, and variable provider expertise. Some patients may be reluctant to disclose over-the-counter or alternative medicine use. Access to medication reviews can be limited in resource-poor settings. Additionally, inconsistent follow-up and lack of integration into electronic health records may reduce effectiveness. These factors can limit the review’s ability to detect and resolve all medication-related problems.

## Common Misconceptions About Medication Reviews
A prevalent misconception is that medication reviews are only necessary if problems arise, when in fact routine reviews prevent complications. Some believe reviews will automatically change their medications, but often the goal is to affirm safe use and provide education. Another misunderstanding is that medication reviews replace physician care; they are complementary. Patients may also confuse medication review with prescription dispensing or insurance formulary checks, which have different purposes.

## Safety Considerations in Medication Reviews
Medication reviews are generally safe and carry minimal risk. Changes to medication should only be made following professional guidance to avoid unintended harm. Reviews help identify unsafe medications, reduce polypharmacy risks, and improve monitoring of side effects. Patients should notify providers of any new symptoms or concerns promptly. It is important to continue regular medical follow-ups in conjunction with medication review recommendations.

## The Role of Medication Reviews in Early Detection of Adverse Effects
Medication reviews facilitate early identification of adverse drug reactions (ADRs) by systematically assessing symptoms, laboratory results, and drug histories. Early detection enables timely intervention which may include dosage adjustment or discontinuation of the offending medication. This proactive approach reduces morbidity and prevents progression to severe complications. Healthcare professionals use standardized tools and checklists during reviews to identify subtle signs of ADRs and interactions.

## Supporting Informed Health Decisions Through Medication Reviews
Medication reviews empower patients by providing clear information about the benefits and risks of their medications. This fosters shared decision-making between patients and healthcare providers, allowing for treatment plans aligned with patient preferences and goals. Improved understanding of medication regimens enhances adherence and satisfaction. Reviews also facilitate discussions about lifestyle modifications and preventive care, contributing to overall health management.

## Practical Guidance
- Keep an updated list of all medications and supplements and bring it to every health visit.
- Be open and honest about your medication use, including over-the-counter and herbal products.
- Ask questions about any concerns, side effects, or difficulties following your medication regimen.
- Schedule regular medication reviews, especially if you take multiple medications or have chronic conditions.
- Follow recommendations from healthcare providers before making any medication changes yourself.

## Safety Notes
- Never alter medication doses or discontinue drugs without consulting a healthcare professional.
- Communicate any new symptoms or side effects promptly during or after a medication review.
- Medication reviews complement but do not replace regular medical care and follow-up.
- Be cautious of non-professional advice regarding medication adjustments.
- Inform all your healthcare providers about completed medication reviews for coordinated care.

## Glossary
- **Medication Review:** A systematic evaluation of a patient's medications aimed at optimizing therapy and improving safety.
- **Polypharmacy:** The use of multiple medications by a patient, often associated with increased risk of adverse effects.
- **Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR):** An unwanted or harmful reaction experienced following the administration of a medication.
- **Medication Therapy Review (MTR):** A synonym for medication review, emphasizing evaluation and management of medication therapy.
- **Medication Reconciliation:** A process ensuring accurate and complete medication information transfer during healthcare transitions.
- **Deprescribing:** The supervised discontinuation or dose reduction of medications that may no longer be needed or may cause harm.
- **Over-the-Counter (OTC) Medications:** Medications available without a prescription, including supplements and herbal products.
- **Medication Adherence:** The extent to which patients take medications as prescribed by their healthcare providers.
