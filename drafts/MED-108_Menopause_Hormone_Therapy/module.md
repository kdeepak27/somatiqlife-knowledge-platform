## Definition
Menopause Hormone Therapy (MHT), also known as hormone replacement therapy (HRT), is the medical use of estrogen alone or combined with progestogen to alleviate symptoms caused by the natural decline of female sex hormones during and after menopause.

## Why This Matters
Many women experience distressing symptoms and increased health risks after menopause due to declining hormone levels. Understanding menopause hormone therapy empowers informed decisions about symptom relief, osteoporosis prevention, and risk management, improving quality of life during midlife and beyond.

## Definition and Terminology of Menopause Hormone Therapy
Menopause Hormone Therapy (MHT), also called hormone replacement therapy (HRT), consists of medications that supplement the female hormones estrogen and progestogen that decline during menopause. Estrogen-only therapy is prescribed for women who have had a hysterectomy. Combined estrogen and progestogen therapy is used for women with an intact uterus to prevent endometrial hyperplasia. Alternate terms include menopausal hormone therapy and postmenopausal hormone therapy. MHT aims to relieve vasomotor symptoms, genitourinary symptoms, and prevent osteoporosis.

## Physiological Changes During Menopause
Menopause typically occurs between ages 45-55 and is characterized by the cessation of menstrual periods due to ovarian follicle depletion leading to diminished estrogen and progesterone production. The lack of estrogen results in changes in the hypothalamic thermoregulatory center causing hot flashes, increased bone resorption raising osteoporosis risk, and urogenital atrophy leading to dryness and urinary symptoms.

## Mechanisms of Action of Menopause Hormone Therapy
MHT works by replacing the declining endogenous estrogen and progestogen hormones. Estrogen stabilizes the hypothalamic thermoregulatory center to reduce vasomotor symptoms and improves vaginal mucosa health. Estrogen inhibits osteoclast-mediated bone resorption, preserving bone density. Progestogens protect the endometrium from estrogen-induced hyperplasia.

## Clinical Benefits of Menopause Hormone Therapy
The primary benefits of MHT include significant reduction of vasomotor symptoms such as hot flashes and night sweats, alleviation of vaginal dryness and dyspareunia, and prevention of osteoporosis and related fragility fractures. MHT may also improve mood and sleep quality; however, evidence is mixed regarding cardiovascular benefits.

## Types and Administration Routes of Hormones in MHT
Hormone formulations include oral tablets, transdermal patches, gels, vaginal rings, and creams. Oral estrogen undergoes first-pass liver metabolism affecting clotting factors. Transdermal delivery avoids first-pass metabolism and may reduce thrombotic risk. Progestogens can be synthetic or natural and vary in potency and side effect profiles.

## Risks and Side Effects Associated With Menopause Hormone Therapy
MHT is associated with increased risks depending on the regimen and patient profile. Risks include breast cancer, venous thromboembolism (VTE), stroke, and cardiovascular events. Estrogen-only therapy in hysterectomized women has a different risk profile than combined therapy. Side effects may include breast tenderness, bloating, mood changes, and spotting.

## Patient Selection and Contraindications for Menopause Hormone Therapy
Ideal candidates are symptomatic women within 10 years of menopause onset or under age 60 without contraindications. Contraindications include known or suspected estrogen-dependent cancers (e.g., breast, endometrial), active liver disease, thromboembolic disorders, unexplained vaginal bleeding, and pregnancy.

## Duration of Therapy and Monitoring Recommendations
MHT should be used at the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration consistent with treatment goals, typically less than 5 years. Regular follow-up includes assessment of symptom control and monitoring for adverse effects. Routine hormone level testing is not typically required. Bone density testing may guide osteoporosis prevention.

## Misconceptions and Public Perceptions about Menopause Hormone Therapy
Common misconceptions include the belief that hormone therapy is unsafe for all women or causes cancer in all cases. Evidence supports that risks vary with age, timing, dose, and hormone type. Many women benefit safely from MHT when appropriately prescribed. Non-hormonal alternatives are not necessarily superior for all symptoms.

## Interactions With Other Medications
Some medications can affect MHT metabolism, including enzyme-inducing drugs (e.g., certain anticonvulsants, rifampin) potentially reducing hormone efficacy. Conversely, MHT may influence metabolism of other drugs. Healthcare providers should review all medications before prescribing MHT.

## Alternatives to Menopause Hormone Therapy for Symptom Management
Non-hormonal options include lifestyle changes, cognitive behavioral therapy, selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs), gabapentin, and vaginal moisturizers or lubricants for genitourinary symptoms. These may be preferred for women with contraindications or personal choice against hormones.

## Practical Guidance
- Discuss menopausal symptoms comprehensively before initiating MHT to determine appropriateness.
- Assess individual risk factors such as age, cardiovascular disease, cancer history, and thromboembolism before prescribing.
- Choose type and route of hormone therapy based on symptom profile, patient preference, and risk.
- Use the lowest effective hormone dose for symptomatic relief and reevaluate regularly.
- Educate patients regarding potential risks and benefits to support shared decision-making.
- Monitor therapy through clinical visits; laboratory monitoring is generally unnecessary unless indicated.
- Consider alternatives if MHT is contraindicated or not preferred.
- Encourage lifestyle measures that support bone health and cardiovascular wellness during therapy.

## Safety Notes
- Avoid MHT in women with active or history of estrogen-dependent cancers.
- Use caution in patients with history of thromboembolism or cardiovascular disease.
- Discontinue MHT if unexplained vaginal bleeding occurs and evaluate promptly.
- Be aware that oral estrogen increases clotting factors more than transdermal routes.
- Regularly reassess the need for continued hormone therapy, aiming for shortest effective duration.

## Common Misconceptions
### Hormone therapy causes breast cancer in all users.
**Reality:** While combined estrogen-progestogen therapy may slightly increase breast cancer risk over long-term use, estrogen-only therapy in hysterectomized women does not show a similar risk. The overall absolute risk varies by individual factors and duration.

### All women should take hormone therapy at menopause.
**Reality:** MHT is intended for symptomatic relief and is not recommended universally. Risks and benefits must be assessed individually based on symptoms, health history, and preferences.

### Hormone therapy prevents heart disease for all women.
**Reality:** Evidence shows cardiovascular effects vary with timing of initiation; starting MHT many years after menopause may increase cardiovascular risks rather than reduce them.

## Clinical Pearls
- Start MHT within 10 years of menopause onset for best benefit-risk balance.
- Transdermal estrogen may be safer for women at higher thrombotic risk.
- Combined therapy is required in women with a uterus to prevent endometrial hyperplasia.
- Use lowest effective dose and reassess need regularly.
- Non-hormonal treatments are effective for some women and should be considered.
- MHT improves quality of life by addressing multiple menopausal symptoms.
- Patient education is crucial for informed consent and adherence.

## Glossary
- **Menopause:** The natural cessation of menstrual periods typically occurring between ages 45 and 55, marked by decreased ovarian hormone production.
- **Estrogen:** A female sex hormone responsible for the development and regulation of the female reproductive system and secondary sexual characteristics.
- **Progestogen:** A class of hormones including progesterone that prepare and maintain the uterus for pregnancy and protect the endometrium during hormone therapy.
- **Vasomotor Symptoms:** Menopause-related hot flashes and night sweats caused by changes in thermoregulatory function due to hormone fluctuations.
- **Hormone Replacement Therapy (HRT):** A medical treatment involving the administration of estrogen alone or combined with progestogen to alleviate menopausal symptoms; synonymous with Menopause Hormone Therapy.
- **Osteoporosis:** A condition where decreased bone density increases fracture risk, commonly occurring after menopause due to estrogen deficiency.
- **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE):** A blood clot that forms in a vein, potentially causing deep vein thrombosis or pulmonary embolism, risk increased by some hormone therapies.
- **Transdermal:** A method of delivering medication through the skin, such as patches or gels, avoiding first-pass liver metabolism.
