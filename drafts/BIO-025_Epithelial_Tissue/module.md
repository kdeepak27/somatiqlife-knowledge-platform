## Definition
Epithelial tissue is a sheet of closely packed cells that covers body surfaces, lines hollow organs and cavities, and forms glands, serving protective, absorptive, secretory, and sensory functions.

## Why This Matters
Epithelial tissue is essential for maintaining the body's internal environment by forming effective barriers, facilitating nutrient and waste exchange, and enabling secretion and sensation. Damage or dysfunction can lead to infections, impaired healing, and diseases such as cancer, making knowledge of epithelial biology critical for health and medical science.

## Structure and Organization of Epithelial Tissue
Epithelial tissue consists of tightly packed cells arranged in continuous sheets. Cells can be organized as single layers (simple epithelium) or multiple layers (stratified epithelium) depending on their protective and functional needs. The apical surface faces the external environment or organ lumen, while the basal surface attaches to an underlying basement membrane. Epithelial cells exhibit polarity with distinct surfaces adapted for specific roles. Cell junctions like tight junctions, adherens junctions, and desmosomes maintain the structural integrity and regulate the passage of substances. The basement membrane provides support and separates epithelial cells from connective tissue.

## Types of Epithelial Tissue
Epithelial tissues are classified by cell shape and layering. Major types include simple squamous epithelium—thin and flat for diffusion; simple cuboidal—cube-shaped for absorption and secretion; simple columnar—tall cells involved in absorption and secretion, often with microvilli or cilia; stratified squamous—multiple layers providing protection; pseudostratified columnar—appearing layered but each cell contacts the basement membrane; and transitional epithelium—adapted to stretch, found in the urinary tract.

## Functions of Epithelial Tissue
Epithelial tissue performs several key functions: protection against physical, chemical, and biological harm; absorption of nutrients and molecules as seen in the intestines; secretion of hormones, enzymes, and mucus from glands; filtration in organs like the kidneys; and sensation by containing specialized nerve cells. The tissue's impermeability and selective permeability are critical to maintaining homeostasis.

## Barrier and Protective Roles
The epithelial barrier shields internal tissues from mechanical injury, pathogens, and harmful chemicals. The stratified epithelium of skin is especially durable, regenerating rapidly to replace damaged cells. Tight junctions prevent leakage between cells, ensuring a controlled environment. When the barrier is compromised, risks of infection and inflammation increase, highlighting the importance of epithelial integrity.

## Epithelial Tissue in Absorption and Secretion
In locations like the intestines and kidneys, simple epithelial layers facilitate the absorption of nutrients, water, and ions. Specialized epithelial cells secrete substances such as enzymes, sweat, and hormones necessary for bodily functions. Structures like microvilli increase surface area to optimize absorption. Glandular epithelium forms exocrine and endocrine glands, playing essential roles in secretion.

## Role of the Basement Membrane
The basement membrane is a thin, dense layer of extracellular matrix underlying epithelial tissue. It anchors the epithelium to connective tissue, provides structural support, and acts as a selective filter regulating molecules passing between epithelium and underlying tissues. It also guides cell migration during wound healing and influences cell differentiation and growth.

## Cell Junctions and Polarity
Epithelial cells are tightly joined by specialized structures: tight junctions prevent fluid leakage between cells; adherens junctions connect the actin cytoskeleton of adjacent cells for stability; desmosomes provide mechanical strength; and gap junctions allow communication. Cell polarity, with distinct apical and basal domains, is key for directional transport and function.

## Clinical Relevance and Common Conditions
Epithelial tissue abnormalities are involved in various diseases. Damage can cause ulcers, infections, and impaired healing. Cancer often arises from epithelial cells, known as carcinomas. Understanding normal epithelial biology aids in interpreting pathological changes, improving diagnosis and treatment. Additionally, epithelial regeneration ability is crucial in recovery after injury.

## Common Misconceptions
### Epithelial tissue only serves as a physical barrier.
**Reality:** Besides serving as a barrier, epithelial tissue also participates in absorption, secretion, sensation, and selective transport.

### All epithelial tissue types are the same throughout the body.
**Reality:** Epithelial tissues vary in structure and function depending on their location and specific roles.

### The basement membrane is a cellular layer.
**Reality:** The basement membrane is an extracellular matrix that supports and separates epithelial tissue from underlying connective tissue.

### Epithelial cells do not regenerate after damage.
**Reality:** Epithelial cells frequently regenerate, especially stratified types like skin epithelium, aiding in tissue repair.

## Clinical Pearls
- Epithelial cells exhibit rapid turnover, making them sensitive indicators of toxic exposures.
- Tight junction dysfunction in epithelia can lead to increased permeability contributing to disease.
- Basement membrane integrity is crucial in preventing invasive cancer spread.
- Recognizing epithelial changes under the microscope is vital for cancer diagnosis.
- Damage to absorptive epithelia in the gut can result in malabsorption syndromes.

## Glossary
- **Epithelial tissue:** A type of tissue composed of closely packed cells covering surfaces and lining cavities in the body.
- **Basement membrane:** A specialized extracellular matrix layer that supports and separates epithelial tissue from underlying tissues.
- **Cell junctions:** Structures that connect epithelial cells, including tight junctions, adherens junctions, desmosomes, and gap junctions.
- **Simple epithelium:** An epithelial layer consisting of a single layer of cells.
- **Stratified epithelium:** An epithelial tissue composed of multiple layers of cells providing protection.
